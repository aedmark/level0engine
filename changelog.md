# Level 0 Engine Changelog

## [v0.6.2] - 2026-08-01

_The Soda Machine & Slow Pupil Update_

### Added
- **[LIGHTING] Atrium Vending Machines Are Now Actual Light Sources:** `buildVendingMachine` created an emissive panel material and stopped there. `LumenGrid` does not discover emitters by scanning the scene — it reads `env.fixtureData`, an explicit registry every other lit prop in the game signs into — so the soda machines were the one emissive surface in the codebase that never registered, and an emissive material in three.js is a surface that *looks* bright while emitting nothing. With `ATRIUM` authored at `ambient: 0.0`, that left the flashlight as the sector's only real illumination. Now registered as a fixture with `distance: 13.0` stated explicitly rather than inherited (`LumenGrid`'s slot defaults drop from 20.0 to 10.0 past the eighth fixture, and a machine that halves its throw when a ninth comes into range reads as a power fault), and `emissiveIntensity: 2.2` passed through — without that override `LumenGrid`'s steady-state branch overwrites the authored glow with its flat `0.4` default on the first frame. The material's `emissive` also moved from `0xffffff` to `0xd8f2ff`: `LumenGrid` copies that uniform tint into the light colour rather than sampling the `emissiveMap`, so pure white was throwing clinical light into a room lit by nothing else.
- **[SOMATICS] Pupil Adaptation:** The glare vignette held at full strength for as long as the player stared at a fixture. Added `Environment.pupilAdapt` (0..1), driven from the smoothed stimulus and applied once on the way out to the shader — deliberately *not* driven from the attenuated value, since feeding the output back into the driver makes each term suppress the other's input and the glare settles into a limit cycle instead of a steady state. Constriction runs at `0.80`/s against dilation's `0.28`/s, preserving the real asymmetry where the pupillary reflex closes down far faster than photopigment recovery in the dark reopens it; the practical result is that looking away from a machine leaves the concourse dimmer than you left it for several seconds. Uses `1 - exp(-rate * dt)` rather than this file's usual fixed lerp coefficient, because a frame-rate-dependent blend is survivable across a quarter-second and not across a multi-second one — an untreated lerp adapts roughly five times faster at 144fps than at 30fps, which would read as a framerate tell rather than a physiological effect. `dt` is derived from the elapsed clock `updateLights` already receives and clamped at 0.1s, since a backgrounded tab returns one enormous stride that would otherwise snap the pupil fully open or shut on the frame the player alt-tabs back in.
- **[SOMATICS] Dark Adaptation Loss (Exposure Dimming):** The second half of the same reflex — an eye stopped down to survive a bright panel has given up the dark. Applied to `toneMappingExposure` rather than the sector ambient for two reasons: exposure is retinal sensitivity, which is what a pupil actually changes, whereas ambient is how much fill light is in the room and staring at a vending machine does not alter that; and more decisively, `ATRIUM` declares `ambient: 0.0`, so scaling it would have done precisely nothing in the one sector the feature was built for. `PUPIL_MAX_DIM` is `0.30` against the glare term's `0.85`, since exposure is global and takes the flashlight, the panels and the floor down with it — roughly half a stop, which reads as your eyes lagging rather than as the lights failing (an event the game already owns via `darknessPressure`).
- **[AUDIO] Atrium Muzak:** The muzak sequencer, chord set and melody already existed but were wired exclusively to `ANNEX`. Extracted the four numbers that actually differ between rooms into a module-scope `MUZAK_PROFILES` table (`gain`, `beat`, `cutoff`, `wobble`) so a third sector is a line of data rather than another arm of a conditional. `ATRIUM` runs `cutoff: 210` against `ANNEX`'s `500`, `beat: 0.72` against `0.50`, and `wobble: 34` against `15` — the same recording, heard across a concourse large enough to swallow it, on a transport that is audibly failing.
- **[PARTICLES] Atrium Dust Profile:** `ATRIUM` had no `dust` block and inherited the defaults. Every value in the new profile is driven by one constraint: the dust cloud is a `PointsMaterial`, which takes no lighting at all, so its `color` *is* its brightness unconditionally. At `ambient: 0.0` a white mote glows exactly as hard standing in pitch darkness as it does in front of a vending machine, which reads as screen dirt rather than atmosphere. Hence the dim cool grey (`0x8fa4b0`), chosen to sit near the vending panels' cast when lit and disappear into the fog when not. Sizes raised from the `0.05`/`0.08` default to `0.16`/`0.20` because a room fourteen balcony tiers tall needs motes large enough to register as suspended in a volume, with `turbulence: 0.45` scattering the settle speeds so the space reads as having moving air in it.
- **[TOOLS] Debug HUD Glare & Pupil Readout:** Added `GLARE` and `PUPIL` lines reporting raw stimulus, smoothed stimulus, shader-facing output, adaptation percentage, live exposure, the camera-to-fixture dot product and the fixture distance. The glare chain has five gates in series — fixture present, distance over 1m, aim inside the `0.95` dot cone, beam alignment, and an occlusion raycast — and when it misbehaves every one of them produces an identical symptom, which makes the failure impossible to attribute by inspection.

### Changed
- **[AUDIO] Spatial Delay Routed Post-Master (Aux Send):** The delay was fed only by `mainGain` — the rumble, whine and kinetic bed, whose gain sits between `0.003` and `0.013`. Everything else in the graph (footsteps, muzak, every foley event, the sector noise beds) connected straight to `masterGain` and never reached it, so each sector's `delay`/`feedback` pair was describing the acoustics of the drone alone. That is why a marble concourse could be authored with the longest tail in the game and still return bone-dry footsteps. Restructured as `masterGain → reverbSend → spatialDelay ⟲ feedbackGain → destination`, which picks up every voice present and future without a source ever having to know the reverb exists, and keeps `setVolume` scaling wet and dry together since `masterGain` sits upstream of the send. The wet return goes to `ctx.destination` rather than back through `masterGain`: that path is legal (WebAudio permits a cycle containing a `DelayNode`) but would stack a second feedback loop on top of `feedbackGain` whose combined gain then varies with the master volume slider. Added a per-sector `wet` send level, defaulting to `0.12` — deliberately low, since a feedback delay has a steady-state gain of `1 / (1 - feedback)` and the CHASM's `0.7` therefore triples whatever enters it.
- **[AUDIO] Atrium Ambient Event Palette:** The sector rolled `'leaves'` at 65% and a doubled `'hoot'` at 35% — rustling undergrowth and a two-note owl call, which is a field at night and not a shopping centre. Replaced with `'page'` at 45% (a PA announcement crackle carrying from somewhere across the concourse) and a `'shuffle'` gait at 55%, running 3–5 steps at roughly 0.5s cadence before stopping mid-crossing. Event spacing widened from 5–15s to 8–24s. Distances now start at `484.0` rather than the `36.0` every other sector uses: `Foley` attenuates by `1 - sqrt(distSq) / 40` and culls past `1600`, so the new band lands 22–33 units out — audible, unplaceable, and never close enough to sound like it is happening to you. The `'leaves'` and `'hoot'` voices remain defined in `Foley.js` for future use.
- **[AUDIO] Atrium Acoustics Retuned:** Noise bed raised from `0.09` to `0.13` so a concourse this size has audible moving air even when nothing else is happening. `delay` cut from `0.55` to `0.24` and `feedback` raised from `0.45` to `0.58` — the original pair was authored while only the drone fed the send, where a long delay merely smears a drone and the spacing is inaudible; once footsteps reach it the repeats become discrete events and `0.55s` is roughly 190m of round trip, a gorge rather than a mall. Short delay with heavy feedback is the closest a single tap gets to a genuine tail. `wet: 0.20`. Note that a single delay line remains an echo and not a reverb; a `ConvolverNode` fed a procedurally generated impulse response would drop into the send slot the delay now occupies.
- **[RENDERING] Soft Shadow Filtering:** `shadowMap.type` moved from `PCFShadowMap` to `PCFSoftShadowMap`. Both variants take nine taps; the soft one weights them by fractional position within the texel rather than snapping to texel centres, so the additional cost is arithmetic rather than bandwidth. Worth recording for future tuning: the PCF kernel is measured in texels rather than world units, which means shadow softness is *inversely* coupled to shadow map resolution — the spot resolution increase below is what made the remaining edges read harder, and the correct next dial if they are still too crisp is down to 1024, not up.
- **[LIGHTING] Spot Shadow Map Resolution Raised to 2048:** `LumenGrid` sized point and spot shadows identically at 512, which prices two very different things the same way. A `PointLightShadow` is a cube — six faces, each covering a clean 90° and none of them distorted. A `SpotLightShadow` is a single 2D map whose camera fov is `2 * light.angle`, so the atrium's vending fixtures, running near the 90° half-angle cap, produce a shadow camera at roughly 167° fov; `tan(83.7°)` is about 9, meaning the frustum spans ±9 units per unit of depth and texel density collapses toward the cone edge. At 512 that quantised into visible combing across every shelf surface. Spots now take 2048 (unchanged at 512 under `shadowQuality: 'low'`, which `LumenGrid` now receives as a constructor argument); points stay at 512, since raising a cube costs six times as much to fix a problem those fixtures never had.
- **[RENDERING] Tone Mapping Exposure Ownership Split:** `toneMappingExposure` is now a derived value with exactly one writer (`Environment.updateLights`), and the player's preference lives in a new `RenderEngine.baseExposure`. The gamma slider, `SaveManager` and the constructor all write the preference; the live value is recomputed from it and the current pupil state every frame. Without the split, each write clobbers the other and whichever ran last in a given frame wins.

### Fixed
- **[WORLD] Atrium Vending Panel Silently Discarded by the Instancer:** The machine body was both parented into `chunkGroup` *and* passed to `addGeometry`, which stages it for `_compileInstances`. That pass batches by geometry+material signature and, on the `InstancedMesh` path, writes only the parent's matrix — child meshes are not carried. A single machine in a chunk fell through to the single-mesh branch and kept its glowing panel; two or more shared a signature, instancing fired, and the panel vanished. The bug scaled with density, so the atrium went darkest precisely where it had the most machines. The body is now parented directly with its collider inserted into the spatial grid by hand, and carries its own `castShadow`/`receiveShadow` flags that `_compileInstances` would otherwise have set.
- **[AUDIO] Muzak Beat Backlog on Sector Re-Entry:** `_muzakNextBeat` counts in `AudioContext` time and keeps advancing while the player is in a sector with no muzak. Re-entering after a few minutes away found the cursor far in the past and scheduled every missed beat at a timestamp that had already gone — WebAudio fires those immediately, so the entire backlog arrived at once. Latent until now because `ANNEX` was the only sector that could arm the scheduler; adding a second made crossing between them routine. The cursor now re-anchors to the present whenever it falls behind.
- **[UI] Saved Gamma Preference Overwritten on Load:** `SaveManager` restored the saved gamma directly into `renderer.toneMappingExposure`, which under the ownership split above is recomputed every frame — the restored value would have survived exactly one tick. Now writes `baseExposure`. Caught while auditing writers for the pupil adaptation work; would have presented as a save/load bug rather than a lighting one.

## [v0.6.1] - 2026-07-31

_The Maintenance & Aesthetics Update_

### Added
- **[UI/UX] Initialization Indicator:** Added a pulsing `INITIALIZING SIMULATION...` text overlay to the initial black boot screen. Since procedural texture generation can take several seconds on integrated GPUs, this provides explicit feedback that the engine is actively working rather than hanging on a frozen screen.
- **[WORLD] Procedural Pegboard Textures:** Added a generated pegboard material for the workbench areas in the Server/Maintenance sectors. Implemented purely procedurally via canvas in `ProceduralTextureFactory.js`, drawing a light brown MDF base dotted with a precise grid of perfectly spaced 1/4" dark holes.

### Changed
- **[WORLD/RENDERING] Annex Sector Wall Z-Fighting (Pinwheel Tiling):** Solved severe Z-fighting on the padded walls in the Annex sector. Walls were previously authored to be slightly longer than their grid cell (`env.cellSize + 0.27`) to avoid gaps at the corners, which caused their front faces to overlap and Z-fight with adjacent door stubs. Switched the generation logic to a mathematical **pinwheel tiling pattern**: walls are now exactly `env.cellSize` (4.0 units) long, but shifted by half their thickness (0.125 units) clockwise. This forces the North/South and East/West walls to abut each other perfectly around the corners, filling outer gaps without ever sharing the same depth coordinates.
- **[LIGHTING] Emissive Overhead Pipes:** Overhead pipes and structural pieces using `rustMat` were glowing uniformly in pitch darkness. The material was authored with a slight `emissive` value (`0x111111`), causing them to glow from the inside out independently of scene lights. Stripped the emissive property so pipes correctly respect environment lighting and fade completely into the shadows.

### Fixed
- **[UI] Menu Button Double-Click Bug:** The control panel menu required clicking the toggle button twice to open on the game's first boot. The toggle logic only checked if an *inline* style had hidden the panel, which evaluated to an empty string on first load since it was hidden via an external CSS class. Updated the button to check the actual `window.getComputedStyle` visibility state so it opens instantly on the first click.

## [v0.6.0] - 2026-07-29

_The Gamma & Stadium Lights Update_

### Added
- **[FX] Impound Stadium Light Volumetric Beams:** Added a visible cone of light through the yard's fog for each stadium mast, using the same trick `ChasmSector`'s lighthouse beam already relies on: a hollow, barely-opaque additive cone (`env._impoundBeamMat`) standing in for volumetric scattering rather than any real light-shaft rendering. Unlike the lighthouse, which re-aims its sweeping beam every frame, the mast's aim is fixed, so it's oriented once via `lookAt` at build time instead of every frame. Iterated down twice after review: opacity from an initial 0.05 to 0.012 and the cone's far-end radius from 6.0 to 4.5 units, since the first pass — compounded by the clustering bug below and the hollow double-sided geometry getting crossed twice per beam by the camera (near wall and far wall both count) — rendered as a solid opaque wedge rather than a haze.

### Changed
- **[LIGHTING] Impound Stadium Lights Widened & Brightened:** At the old default cone (`spotAngle` left unset, falling back to `LumenGrid`'s generic `Math.PI / 8`) and `baseIntensity: 5.5`, the masts read narrower and dimmer than the player's own flashlight. `spotAngle` now set explicitly to `Math.PI / 4` (double the default, and wider than the flashlight's `Math.PI / 7`) with a `0.6` `spotPenumbra` for a soft floodlight edge instead of a tight spotlit circle, and `baseIntensity` raised to `8.0` — accounting for `LumenGrid`'s `0.35` non-shadow-slot intensity scalar, that nets roughly 2.8 effective versus the flashlight's peak of 2.2, where the old value only ever cleared about 1.9.

### Fixed
- **[RENDERING] Post-Processing Pipeline Never Applied Color-Space Encoding:** Two related bugs surfaced from the same report. First, `RenderEngine.js`'s `renderer.outputColorSpace = THREE.SRGBColorSpace` was a straight no-op on this build (three.js r128 predates both; the property doesn't exist on the renderer and the enum doesn't exist on `THREE`), silently assigning `undefined` onto a property the renderer never reads. Feature-detected instead — falls back to r128's `outputEncoding`/`sRGBEncoding` when `outputColorSpace` isn't present on the renderer, mirroring the same capability-check pattern already used for `compileAsync`. Second, and the actual reason nothing visibly changed after that fix: the main scene renders into an offscreen `WebGLRenderTarget` whose `texture.encoding` is correctly left `Linear` (an intermediate buffer should stay linear), but the only draw call that ever targets the screen is `postMaterial` — a hand-written `ShaderMaterial` for the CRT/somatic distortion pass — and three.js only auto-appends the sRGB encode (`#include <encodings_fragment>`) to shaders it generates for built-in materials, never to a raw `ShaderMaterial`'s own GLSL. The game had never applied a real gamma curve to anything displayed, ever; the "Gamma" slider (actually wired to `toneMappingExposure`, default 1.2) was the only thing standing in for it, which is why cranking it made shadows/midtones look better but blew out anything already bright (vent grilles, door highlights) — a flat exposure multiplier can't reproduce an actual sRGB curve's shape. Added a `linearToSRGB` GLSL function (matching three.js's own formula) as the final step of `postMaterial`'s fragment shader, and applied the same per-texture `colorSpace`/`encoding` feature-detection in `ProceduralTextureFactory.js`'s asset-finishing pass (`markSRGB`) for the equivalent bug on texture inputs (diffuse/emissive maps).
- **[WORLD] Default Zone Fog Ignoring Configured Density:** `Environment._resolveActiveSector` hardcoded `let targetFog = 0.05;` as the starting value for the `NORMAL` sector case, only overwriting it by looking up a sector's config when the player stood inside one of the bounded macro zones (`IMPOUND`, `BOARDROOM`, etc.) — `NORMAL` isn't one of those, it's the fallback for everywhere else, so `SECTORS.NORMAL.fog` was never actually read while in the default zone regardless of what it was set to. Changed to `this._sectorFog("NORMAL")`, so setting `NORMAL.fog` to `0` in `Sectors.js` now actually produces zero fog there. Note the fog slider is a multiplier on a sector's authored density, not an additive override, so it still has no effect in a zone authored at exactly zero.
- **[LIGHTING] Impound Stadium Lights Not Illuminating Anything:** `LumenGrid.update` hardcoded every non-shadow-casting light's falloff `distance` purely by pool-slot category, ignoring the `distance: 35.0` the masts explicitly authored — since they're `noShadow: true`, they always land in a category that got force-set to `10.0` instead. The mast sits 7.5 units up and aims 10 units out, so the straight-line distance to the ground it's meant to light is roughly 12.5 — past that 10-unit cutoff, so nothing in the cone ever received any light regardless of angle or intensity. `light.distance` now respects a fixture's own `distance` field when set, falling back to the pool-slot category default otherwise. A second, related instance of the same bug (`activeRadius`, which gates how far the *camera* can be from a fixture before its pooled light fades to nothing — a different distance than the light's own throw range) was found immediately after and fixed the same way, since the mast's lit target sits almost exactly at the edge of the old hardcoded radius.
- **[LIGHTING] Impound Stadium Light Panels Looking Unlit:** `LumenGrid`'s steady-state branch hardcoded a fixture's own glow (`material.emissiveIntensity`) to a flat `0.4` regardless of how bright its actual light source was — fine while every non-lighthouse fixture threw roughly the same amount of light, but once the masts were bumped to a much brighter `baseIntensity` (see Changed, above), their panel prop stayed exactly as dim as an ordinary ceiling light while throwing a much stronger beam, reading as an unlit bulb next to its own light. Added a per-fixture `emissiveIntensity` override (falls back to the old flat `0.4` for every fixture that doesn't set one); the masts now use `2.5`.
- **[RENDERING] Impound Stadium Light Panels Invisible Through Fog:** The mast panel's material was subject to scene fog like any ordinary surface, so at range it blended into the haze instead of reading as a distinct point of light — fog realistically scatters *reflected* light, not light coming directly from an emitter toward the camera, which is why a real light stays visible through mist while the matte surface around it disappears. Set `fog: false` on the panel material so it now holds full brightness regardless of distance.
- **[LIGHTING] Impound Stadium Lights Clustering:** The mast placement roll (`random() > 0.88`, ~12% per eligible open-floor cell) had no spacing check at all, so nothing stopped several adjacent cells — only 4 units apart — from all succeeding and piling multiple masts on top of each other. Tagged mast fixtures with `isImpoundMast: true` and rejected any new placement candidate within 32 units of an existing one, checked directly against `env.fixtureData` — that array is already pruned the moment its owning chunk unloads, so it doubles as a self-cleaning registry with no new bookkeeping required.
- **[FX] Backup Daemon Cable Trap — Excessive Camera Roll:** `_zapPlayer`'s disorientation effect added a flat 0.25-0.5 radian (~14-29 degree) kick straight to `camera.rotation.z` on every zap. `PlayerController`'s per-frame roll damping does pull this back toward level on its own, but combined with the position teleport and the `somatic-breaker` flinch firing in the same instant, a kick that large read as being thrown into a spin rather than a jolt. Reduced to 0.05-0.12 radians.

## [v0.5.15] - 2026-07-29

_The Live Wire & Serpent Update_

### Added
- **[ENTITIES] The Backup Daemon (Server Hazard):** A brand-new hazard exclusive to the SERVER sector, replacing no prior entity — Server previously ran on the generic Anomaly fallback. Rides the ceiling's hanging cables like a live circuit: it tracks the player's actual frame-to-frame heading (not just their current position), projects a point 9 units ahead along that heading, and steers toward it at speed 7.5 so it reads as cutting the player off rather than trailing behind. Continuously lights up to two nearby un-lit hanging cables (`MAX_LIT`) within range of its current position, swapping each to a bright energized material; touching a lit cable (a small contact radius plus 1.6 units of vertical tolerance, so it doesn't require a pixel-perfect hit) zaps the player to a safe random coordinate — up to 24 candidate spots at growing radii, each checked against loaded chunks and real collision geometry via the spatial grid, so it can never strand the player mid-air, inside a wall, or in an unloaded chunk — and reuses the existing `'breaker'` Foley voice for the shock.
- **[FX] Backup Daemon Cable Sparks:** Iterated three times against direct feedback. First pass added a genuine spark/glow effect in place of a flat white material swap on contact. Second pass recolored the sparks yellow and replaced their orbit-forever behavior with real gravity — they fall and land on the floor beneath wherever the lit cable is. Final pass reorganized independent per-shard respawning into synchronized bursts: a pooled slot per lit cable launches all of its shards together in one shared random direction each cycle, waits out a random 0.6-1.8 second interval once every shard has landed, then launches again in a fresh random direction — reads as a cable spitting a distinct burst of sparks rather than a continuous shower.
- **[WORLD] Server Sector Hanging Cable System:** Added scattered ceiling cables across the Server sector using three drop styles — straight drops, loosely sagging drops (`CatmullRomCurve3` + `TubeGeometry` for genuine slack instead of a rigid angled cylinder), and shallow catenary "swags" between two points — pulled from an eight-color material palette so a chunk's cables don't all repeat the same look. Each cable registers into a per-chunk `env.hangingCables` registry (position, mesh, material, chunk hash) that the Backup Daemon above reads from directly, and is pruned automatically on chunk unload the same way the rest of the per-chunk registries are.
- **[WORLD] Server Sector Perimeter Dressing:** Server's perimeter walls previously fell back to plain wallpaper on every non-doorway cell. Added workbenches and filing cabinets as dedicated wall dressing instead, classified against the same doorway/shoulder/corner logic `buildPerimeter` already uses so they only ever place against genuinely plain wall cells.

### Changed
- **[GEOMETRY] Server Cable Spools & A/V Carts Enlarged:** Both props read as built for Hobbits at their original scale. Cable spool cap/core radii and A/V cart shelf/leg/CRT dimensions all scaled up to a size that reads as real industrial equipment.
- **[AESTHETICS] Server Light Fixture — Borrowed From Checkpoint:** Server's flat panel light replaced with Checkpoint's cage-tube fixture (wire guard, end brackets, cross bars), recolored red (`0xff3333`/`0xff0000`) to match the sector's existing red light scheme. `_buildCheckpointCageLight`'s signature grew three optional trailing parameters (color, emissive, intensity, all defaulting to Checkpoint's original values) so the one fixture builder now serves both sectors without duplicating geometry.
- **[RENDERING] Server Racks & Ceiling — Reflective Glare Removed:** The point lights reflecting off Server's racks and ceiling didn't correspond to any actual physical light structure, reading as a glare with nothing visibly causing it. Rather than mutating `env.serverFloorMat` directly (it's shared with Maintenance's own floor), cloned it into a new `env.serverCeilingMat` with `metalness` zeroed and `roughness` raised to 0.95, and applied the same tuning to the rack material — killing the specular hotspot outright instead of just dimming it.
- **[ENTITIES] Anomaly Suppressed Inside Sectors:** The Anomaly's proximity-based visual-distortion pressure was a pure Euclidean distance check with no concept of walls, so it could sit in the connecting hallway on the other side of a sector's perimeter and still visually read as stalking through it. `Anomaly.update()` now takes the player's active sector and short-circuits (still animates in place, just senses, chases, and pushes zero pressure) whenever that sector isn't `'NORMAL'`, picking back up the instant the player steps back out into the plain maze.
- **[ENTITIES] Incinerator "Ember" — Full Body Redesign:** The Ember's icosahedron core plus twelve orbiting box "slag chunks" was, once compared side by side, nearly an exact recolor of the Anomaly's own icosahedron-core-plus-orbiting-tetrahedra body — the two entities read as reskins of each other. Replaced with a genuine flame body: a chain of nine tapered cone "licks" in three heat-tiered materials (dark red base, orange mid, hot yellow tip), individually flickering and swaying rather than orbiting. Iterated further per feedback into its current form: the chain now lays along the ground and slithers using an actual traveling sine wave (each segment repeats the wave the one ahead of it made, delayed by a phase offset proportional to its distance from the head, with lateral amplitude widening toward the tail so it lashes wider than the head does) driven by the entity's real frame-to-frame movement heading rather than a fixed orientation; gained an anglerfish-style lure (a stalk arching off the head with a glowing bulb that burns brighter the longer the player has been staring) and a face (two pulsing white-hot eyes and seven staggered dark fangs, both tracked independently of the head cone's own flicker so they don't warp with it); and was resized twice more (halved, then walked back up twice) to its current 0.5 scale factor on a dedicated `bodyGroup` wrapper that scales the whole visual — flame, lights, embers, face, lure — without touching the unscaled locomotion/collision math underneath it.
- **[FX] Incinerator Ember Embers:** Reused the Backup Daemon's synchronized burst-cycle spark mechanic (see above) for the Ember's own ambient ember spit, recolored to a warm amber and tied to a single always-on emission point at the flame's core rather than the Daemon's per-cable pool; burst frequency scales up with `heatLevel`, so it sparks more violently the longer it's been stared at.
- **[ENTITIES] Incinerator Ember Speed Rework:** Base pursuit speed dropped from a flat 7.0 to 1.4 — a slow, patient prowl rather than full-tilt pursuit at all times. Its actual speed potential now comes almost entirely from `heatLevel` (multiplier raised from ×1 to ×1.4), which only accumulates while the player is directly staring at it, so a long stare banks a genuinely fast strike instead of a flat pursuit speed that was always the same regardless of player behavior.

### Fixed
- **[LIGHTING] Cross-Sector Light Bleed Through Perimeter Walls:** `LumenGrid`'s fixed light pool selected fixtures by pure distance with no wall or sector awareness, so a fixture from a neighboring sector could light the floor visibly through a perimeter wall. Added a chunk-hash equality gate — non-lighthouse fixtures outside the player's current chunk are skipped entirely — so a sector's lighting can no longer bleed through its own walls.
- **[WORLD] Server Perimeter Showing Yellow Wallpaper:** Two rounds of the same underlying bug. First, `ServerSector.js` was passing `env.sharedWallMat` (the yellow wallpaper reserved for outside the sectors) as `buildPerimeter`'s inward-facing wall material; corrected to `env.structMat`. Second, once workbenches/filing cabinets were added as dressing, they were spawning at an inset (1.5 units) that landed them on the outer, undressed ring rather than against the sector's own interior wall face — `pushWallSegment` builds full cell-width solid wall blocks, not thin planes, so the inset needed to clear the wall's own footprint entirely; raised to `(cellSize / 2) + 0.45`.
- **[WORLD] Checkpoint Hazmat Suits Floating/Misoriented:** Suit legs sat with an 0.08-unit gap below the torso (fixed leg Y position); some suits faced the wall instead of out, from an inverted `faceYaw` ternary for non-`alongZ` rack orientations (branches swapped); and suits had no visible connection to their rack, appearing to float — added a hook mesh bridging the suit's hood to the rail above it.
- **[WORLD] Incinerator Overhead Vents Read as Plain Columns, Z-Fought With Fixtures:** The ceiling duct "risers" were flat textured boxes with no actual vent geometry, reading as bare metal columns rather than vents. Rebuilt as a proper vent assembly (shaft, a distinct flanged collar at the joint, and a wide ember-lit grille cap at the bottom) with every joint given 0.07-0.22 units of real overlap instead of the old 0.02-0.025 unit hairline gaps that were causing the z-fighting against the ember-grille "light fixture" panels sitting almost exactly coplanar with the duct spine above them.
- **[WORLD] Incinerator Vent Risers Clipping Through Walls:** Each riser hangs 1.65 units off-center with a 0.43-unit collar half-width, reaching 2.08 units — past the 2.0-unit cell boundary — regardless of whether the neighboring cell was open corridor or a solid maze wall block. Now checks the actual wall booleans for that cell and skips whichever side faces a wall; the sector's own connectivity logic guarantees at least one side stays open, so this never drops both risers.

- **[PERFORMANCE] Impound Hard Lag Spikes — Unthrottled Warden LOS:** `WardenEntity._updateSenses` ran its occlusion raycast every single frame, unthrottled, querying the spatial grid at up to a 30-unit radius and ray-testing every `isEntityBlocker` box returned. Impound generates far more of that box type than any other sector (fence segments on nearly every perimeter cell, plus cars, machines, and tire stacks), so that query's candidate set was consistently the largest in the game, recomputed 60 times a second regardless. `Anomaly.js` had already solved this exact cost for its own LOS check by re-running it a few times a second and caching the result between checks; the Warden never got the same treatment. Brought in line with the same `_lastLOSTime` / `_lastLOSResult` throttle.
- **[ENTITIES] Warden Searchlight Aimed at Its Own Feet:** The spotlight's sweep target only ever offset 1 unit horizontally against a light mounted 3.8 units up — roughly 15 degrees off straight down regardless of sweep phase, so it never actually read as scanning, just as pointed at the ground under itself. Widened the sweep target radius to 10 units for a genuine scanning angle across the floor.
- **[ENTITIES] Warden Freezing on Fence Contact:** `_resolveLocomotion`'s blocked-movement handling had cases for blocked-on-X-only and blocked-on-Z-only, but no fallback at all for blocked-on-both — a case Impound's dense fencing makes common. Hitting it meant the position update was silently skipped every frame, forever: a permanent freeze with no recovery path. Added the same random-nudge escape `Anomaly.js` already uses for its equivalent case.
- **[PERFORMANCE] Impound Hard Engine Stops While Moving — LumenGrid Shadow-Slot Churn:** `LumenGrid` maintains a fixed 8-slot pool for real-time shadow-casting lights and re-picks the 8 nearest fixtures every frame; a slot can represent either a SpotLight or a PointLight depending on which fixture currently owns it, and flipping that type changes the scene's active shadow-light-type counts, forcing a WebGL shader-program recompile. Impound's floodlight masts spawn on ~12% of interior cells (15-20 per chunk, far denser than any other sector) and are all shadow-eligible SpotLights, so running through a cluster reassigned — and flipped the type of — shadow slots faster than the existing per-frame reassignment cap (see v0.5.11) could smooth out on its own. Flagged the masts with LumenGrid's existing `noShadow` escape hatch (present in the code but previously unused anywhere), pulling them out of the shadow-slot competition entirely; they still light the yard as bright directional spots, just without casting their own dynamic shadow.
- **[WORLD] Impound Airlock's Corrugated-Metal Awning:** `buildEntranceHallways` caps every sector's entrance approach with a flat ceiling plane at a fixed ~3-unit height, and swaps in a dedicated corrugated-metal material for Impound specifically. Harmless in a normal room, but Impound's actual ceiling sits 20 units up, so the cap hung low directly over the airlock like a literal sheet of corrugated metal acting as an awning against the rest of the yard's open height. Excluded IMPOUND from that hallway ceiling cap, the same way ARCHIVE already was for its own tall ceiling — the entrance now opens straight up into the yard instead.

## [v0.5.11] - 2026-07-28

_The Frame Budget Update_

### Fixed
- **[PERFORMANCE] Boot-Time Texture Generation Freeze:** `Environment.setup()` called `ProceduralTextureFactory.generateAssets()` synchronously — eleven asset-group builder functions drawing 30+ procedural canvases back-to-back with zero yields — and did so *before* the spawn flash overlay was ever raised, so the very first thing a fresh page load produced was an unresponsive frame, not the intended flash-to-black-and-fade-in. `generateAssets()` is now `async` and yields (`setTimeout(...,0)`) between each asset group, mirroring the yield pattern already used for chunk streaming in `processChunkQueue`. `Environment.setup()` now raises the flash overlay and yields one tick — letting the browser actually paint it — before awaiting texture generation, so the overlay masks the load instead of the load blocking the overlay. `main.js` awaits `setup()` via top-level await so nothing downstream runs against a half-initialized environment.
- **[PERFORMANCE] Resolution Scale Ignoring Saved Settings on Cold Boot:** `RenderEngine`'s constructor hardcoded `resolutionScale = 2.0` (2x internal supersampling, 4x the pixel fill cost) regardless of what the settings menu showed. Returning players had this silently overwritten a moment later by `SaveManager.loadState()`, but a first-time session with no `level0_state` in `localStorage` never had that value touched — so new players rendered at 2x resolution scale for the entire session despite the "Internal Resolution" dropdown reading "100% (Native)". `RenderEngine` now reads the last resolution the player explicitly chose directly out of `localStorage` at construction time (`getSavedResolutionScale()`), independent of `SaveManager`'s own initialization order, falling back to 1.0 — matching the dropdown's own default — when no save exists yet.
- **[PERFORMANCE] Shadow Map Reassignment Bursts:** `LumenGrid` re-prioritizes its 8 shadow-casting slots by proximity every frame; whenever a fixture newly took over a slot — which happens naturally while moving, as the "closest 8 lights" set changes membership — it forced that light's shadow map to re-render immediately and unconditionally, with no cap on how many of the 8 slots could reassign (and therefore force a same-frame shadow pass) at once. Profiling via injected frame-level instrumentation during an active Anomaly chase near the Archive sector traced the game's worst frame-time spikes directly to this: frames with 3+ simultaneous slot reassignments averaged 31.4ms of `render()` time (p90 39.6ms) against a 10.0ms baseline for churn-free frames, accounting for nearly all of the session's frames slower than 33ms — and reported by players as noticeable light pop-in. `LumenGrid.update()` now caps forced shadow-map updates to 2 per frame (`maxForcedShadowUpdatesPerFrame`); any additional slots that reassign in the same frame are queued (`_pendingShadowSlots`) and drained through the existing round-robin refresh over the next frame or two instead of all stacking onto the current one. Re-profiling the identical repro afterward showed the 3+ churn bucket's p90 render time drop to 19.9ms, with frames slower than 33ms falling from 35 to 2 over a comparable capture window.

Reworked into a single continuous curve: darkness-driven drain now only begins past `perceivedDarkness > 0.4` (genuine darkness, not merely being off to the side of a working light) and eases in quadratically from that floor, so there is no longer any darkness value at which recovery becomes permanently unreachable.

- **[ENTITIES] Anomaly Sector-Routing Staleness:** `Environment.updateEntity()` (called early in the frame from `main.js`) read `_stickySectorId` before `updateLights()` — the only place that computed it — ran later in that same frame, so entity routing (which hazard is active) always worked off the *previous* frame's sector classification. - **[ENTITIES] Sector-Locked Hazards Escaping Their Territory:** The Warden and the Ember pursue with a raw `target.copy(playerPos)` and actively force open any interactive door within range while chasing, with no awareness of their own sector's boundaries — a determined chase (or a door left open) could walk either straight out into the hallway, directly into the Anomaly's own territory. - **[RENDERING] Distance Z-Fighting:** `WebGLRenderer`'s `logarithmicDepthBuffer` option existed in `RenderEngine.js` but was gated behind an undocumented `?logdepth` URL flag, off by default.

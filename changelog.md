# Level 0 Engine Changelog

## [v0.9.4] - 2026-08-11

_Airlock Stutter & Anomaly Boundaries_

### Changed

- **[WORLD] Freestanding Walls Use Native Builder:** Replaced raw `BoxGeometry` instancing in `WallBreach.js` with the engine's internal `buildWall` routine. This guarantees that wallpaper UVs correctly scale and tile across the entire length of dynamically generated wall stubs instead of aggressively stretching or compressing the texture.
- **[WORLD] Ride Queue Pylons Link Into Velvet Ropes:** The `RideQueueHall` generator's random standalone pylons have been updated. They now spawn consecutively and connect to each other with drooping red velvet ropes, transforming a field of bizarre metal posts into a cohesive theme-park-style switchback queue.
- **[ENTITY] Anomaly Respects Sector Boundaries:** The anomaly's navigation bounds have been updated to avoid the footprint of *all* 11 major sectors (previously it only respected 3). Additionally, if the player enters a sector, the anomaly will immediately despawn and teleport away, naturally respawning via distance checks only once the player returns to the normal backrooms.

### Fixed

- **[WORLD] Airlock Doors Stop Stuttering For Entities:** Fixed a critical state machine loop in `InteractionController.js` where an entity trying to open an airlock while the player was far away would cause the door to repeatedly transition between "Opening" and "Closing" on every frame. The door now correctly stays open as long as the entity is present, resolving a loud, stochastic repeating sound effect.
- **[WORLD] Entities Cannot Force Airlocks Open:** Entities (such as the Anomaly, Warden, and Incinerator) no longer treat airlock doors as generic interactable sliders. They will ignore them entirely when looking for doors to push through, ensuring airlocks remain strictly manual barriers.
- **[WORLD] Airlock Doors Don't Ignore Player Input While Closing:** Due to the flutter bug above, clicking an airlock door while it was in the `CLOSING_AFTER_EXIT` state would completely discard the player's interaction. The state machine now allows a closing door to immediately reverse course and reopen if interacted with.

## [v0.9.3] - 2026-08-10

_Structural Guarantees & Narrative Exhaustion_

### Changed

- **[VISUALS] Retro FPS Weapon Aesthetics:** The `PaintballGun` model has been stripped of its distracting on-screen hand geometry to achieve a classic, minimalist DOOM-style appearance. The lower receiver (grip and trigger) has been retained but the entire weapon `basePos` has been shifted downwards on the screen (from Y `-0.22` to `-0.35`) so that the empty grip sits cleanly out of frame, focusing the player's view entirely on the barrel and slide mechanics.
- **[VISUALS] Lowered Compass Interface:** The `Compass` instrument has been adjusted to sit lower on the player's screen (Y shifted from `-0.235` to `-0.32`), creating a more flush alignment with the newly lowered weapon layout and freeing up central screen real estate.

### Fixed

- **[WORLD] Duct Networks No Longer Tunnel Into Occupied Space:** The `DuctOrVent` blueprint builds its layout via a breadth-first search into adjacent wall cells. However, it was indiscriminately claiming cells that had already been processed and built upon by the chunk manager in earlier iterations. This resulted in duct geometry projecting directly through solid wall blocks and other solid structures. The BFS now explicitly respects the `ctx.isOccupied` state of adjacent cells, treating them as dead ends and properly capping the vent run with a metal grate.
- **[WORLD] Starting Chamber Is Guaranteed An Exit Route:** The generator clears a 5x5 spawn chamber at `(0, 0)`, but relied entirely on the chaotic noise algorithm to coincidentally leave a navigable gap connecting this chamber to the primary arterial paths radiating from the chunk center. A bad noise roll could generate a solid perimeter wall that completely sealed the starting room. The chunk manager now enforces a manual `carvePath` umbilical from `(7, 7)` directly down to `(0, 0)` in the starting chunk to guarantee connectivity.
- **[WORLD] Wall Breaches Align Perpendicular To Walls:** The geometric axis validation inside the `WallBreach` logic was mathematically inverted. When projecting a crawlspace tunnel through a wall, the breach was orienting its opening parallel to the wall rather than perpendicular to it. This caused the grates to project uselessly against solid stone while leaving the traversable pathway obstructed by door frames. The `isRotated` check has been inverted to correctly bisect the host wall.
- **[WORLD] Airlock Apron Suppresses Floating Stairs:** While airlock aprons correctly cleared out divider walls, the clearance left empty cells that were susceptible to generating a `CratesOrStairway` blueprint. Because the surrounding walls were cleared, this resulted in stairs generating in the middle of open space with their side collisions turned off. The chunk manager now intercepts this fallback near airlock aprons and replaces it with standard solid wall generation to seal the gaps.
- **[NARRATIVE] Story Engine Escalate Ephemera Pools:** When a sector exhausted its native pool of side-lore (clipboards, laptops, notes), the narrative engine resorted to mathematically wrapping the index back to zero, dealing the exact same document multiple times. The `StoryEngine` now falls back to the global `DEFAULT` pool when a sector pool runs dry, and issues blank `[ MISSING PAPERWORK ]` or `[ NO SIGNAL ]` documents if the default pool is also exhausted, guaranteeing no duplicates.
- **[UI] Side-Lore Documents Omit Case File Footer:** The `DocumentViewer` was unconditionally rendering the `DATA RECOVERED: [X / Y]` progress tracker at the bottom of all inspectable documents, falsely giving the impression that reading side-lore was failing to increment the case file count. The viewer now recognizes the `ephemera`, `laptop`, and `clipboard` flags and explicitly hides the tracker footer for texts that are not part of the primary case file total.

## [v0.9.2] - 2026-08-10

_Environmental Polishing & Duct Networks_

### Added

- **[WORLD] Ducts Feature Dynamic Branching Networks:** The duct structural profile now utilizes a Depth-First Search carving algorithm to generate branching, continuous duct networks up to 15 tiles long. A secondary pass guarantees that all branches lead to at least two valid exit grates; if a network gets trapped, it automatically prunes itself down to a single decorative wall vent instead. 

### Changed

- **[AUDIO] Incinerator Entity Slithers:** The incinerator entity no longer uses clanking mechanical glitch sounds. It has been updated to use the existing `ember_slither` Foley audio to better match its visual identity as a snake made of flame.
- **[WORLD] Hallway Chains Suffer Fewer Dead Ends:** Procedural doorway runs dead-ended nearly 50% of the time, making navigation frustrating. The terminus logic now heavily favors alternative exits (dropping dead ends to just 5%), and adds `crevice` to the pool of possible emergency exits alongside ducts, breaches, and crawlspaces.

### Fixed

- **[WORLD] Duct Networks Delete Intersecting Default Walls:** Because chunks generate cells sequentially, a duct network branching "backward" into a previously generated cell would collide with the already-placed solid wall geometry. The chunk manager now tags default walls during staging and dynamically strips them out if a multi-cell structure expands to claim their space.
- **[AESTHETICS] Duct Interiors Prevent Baseboard Bleed:** Baseboards automatically attach to any geometry flagged as a sector wall. Inner duct linings were unintentionally carrying this flag, causing wooden trim to generate inside metal vents. The duct builder now intercepts its local geometry commits and scrubs the flag.
- **[WORLD] Crawlspaces and Breaches Fix Baseboard Z-Fighting:** The baseboards along the drop-ceilings of crawlspaces and breaches were Z-fighting with the yellow wallpaper above them. The baseboards have been explicitly nudged down slightly and use the dedicated trim material rather than blending with the ceiling.
- **[WORLD] Airlock Aprons Clear Divider Walls:** Airlock aprons now properly suppress the random generation of floor-to-ceiling divider walls, ensuring the approach corridors remain unobstructed.
- **[AESTHETICS] Clinic Beds Have Support Rails:** The metal rails on the clinic beds were previously floating in mid-air. They now have vertical support bars anchoring them to the main bedframe.


## [v0.9.1] - 2026-08-09

_What's Behind The Door_

### Added

- **[WORLD] Doors Open Onto Somewhere:** A hinged doorway used to carve a 2–4 × 2–4 room behind itself and wall its border with `ctx.setWall(px, pz, true)` — but it never *forced* a structure on those border cells, so when the build loop reached them they rolled the full structural matrix and landed on `WIDE HEADER GAP`, `NARROW HEADER GAP`, `PARTITION HEADER`, `DUCT OR VENT`, `TUNNEL BURST` and friends, most of which punch an opening. The room was sealed in the grid and then perforated by its own border geometry, which is why a door read as decoration: it opened into a pocket that opened straight back into the world. Behind a door is now a winding one-cell-wide corridor of 4–8 cells that turns every few steps, with alcove recesses hanging off the outside of its corners, and every neighbouring cell forced to `"SOLID FILL"` — a name deliberately matching no profile, so `structuralMatrix.find` returns undefined and `ChunkManager`'s fallback builds plain solid wall. A run ends one of three ways: a dead end, another door chained deeper (budget of two extra links, and only when a second run actually fits), or a rejoin to the maze via `CRAWLSPACE_HALL`, `breach` or `DUCT OR VENT`. Measured across 4,000 simulated chunks: 5.76 doors per chunk, runs averaging just under 6 cells with 1.26 turns, alcoves on 63% of runs.
- **[WORLD] Doorways Face All Four Directions:** Every door in the game faced +Z, and the reason was never the door system — `InteractionController` computes its swing entirely relative to `door.userData.closedRot`, infers the approach axis from it, and `BoardroomSector.hangDoor` has been passing a real `closedRot` for its vestibule doors all along. The constraint was the carve: `ChunkManager` iterates x-major, so a blueprint building from inside the loop could only ever claim cells the cursor hadn't reached yet — the half-plane ahead — which pinned the corridor, and therefore the door, to +Z. Doorway layout now happens in the chunk pre-pass alongside the airlock apron and the `forcedOpen` connectivity walk, before any geometry is staged, so a run is free to head any direction. `HingedDoorway` is now purely geometric: it reads its facing from `ctx.getDoorwayPlan(x, z)`, builds through a `toWorld` helper, and hands `rot` to the door as `closedRot` so the existing inference picks the Z approach for ±Z facings and the X approach for ±X. Distribution across 23,050 planted doors: 26.1% +Z, 28.1% +X, 23.9% −Z, 21.9% −X.
- **[WORLD] Grated Crawl Breaches Run Deeper And Seal At Both Ends:** The breach variant with the lattice grate was a single cell with one grate on one face, leaving the far side of the hole permanently open. It now spans 1–3 cells along the travel axis (72% / 16.9% / 11.2%, so it still reads as a hole punched through a wall rather than a corridor in its own right) with a grate at each mouth. `addGrate` gained an `openSign` option because the second panel would otherwise hinge the same way as the first and swing back into the crawl instead of out of it. Extension only claims cells the generator already left empty and stops at the first wall — carving new ones would hand the pathfinder routes it never planned — and is clamped to the chunk, since geometry stages under the chunk's hash and a segment crossing the boundary would unload on this chunk's schedule while the neighbour rebuilt over it.
- **[TOOLING] One VAR Screen:** `parameters.json` keeps `VARS` (expressions over `ctx`) and `CORE_VARS` (`{min,max}` numbers rolled per playthrough) as separate keys because the engine genuinely treats them differently, but that split was never a useful thing for an author to hold in their head — `CORE_VARS` didn't even match `isVarsObj` in `rendering.js`, so it fell through to a raw JSON textarea while `VARS` got the proper key/value editor. Selecting either now opens one merged list: engine built-ins (`P`, `pen`, `year`, `hours`, `seed`) as locked rows with their resolved preview, then everything you've defined. You don't pick which kind you're creating — a range like `10-99` or `10..99` is stored as a rolled number, anything else as an expression, and editing an entry from one form to the other migrates it between stores. `CORE_VARS` no longer appears as a separate navigation entry.

### Changed

- **[AESTHETICS] Chairs And Couches Get Wooden Legs:** `buildChair` and `buildCouch` built their legs from `env.structMat` — the streaked concrete surface used for foundations, ceilings and perimeter walls, at roughness 1.0 — while `buildTable` and `buildDesk` two functions below already used `env.woodMat`. All eight legs now match the tables. `woodMat` is a shared material so there's no extra draw call. The checkpoint bench and the clinic waiting bench were left alone: the first is concrete-on-metal by design and the second was already an all-metal frame with vinyl pads.
- **[WORLD] The Crawlspace Marks Itself With Trim, Not Tape:** The low-clearance corridor cell signalled its dropped soffit with a flat `0xffdd00` stripe floating in the opening near the top edge, which read as a warning decal stuck onto a bug rather than a finished edge. The soffit's bottom edge is where the wall stops, so it now gets the same baseboard the walls get where they stop at the floor — body from 1.2 to 1.3875 in `env.baseboardMat`, trim capping it to 1.4109, both lifted from `addGeometry`'s baseboard so it's the identical profile at the identical 0.03 protrusion. The floor plainly carries on underneath; you just have to go down to follow it. Hazard tape is now gone from the engine entirely.
- **[WORLD] The Breach Grate Is Shut, And Swings:** The lattice grate in a wall breach was baked in at `rotation.y = Math.PI * 0.42`, permanently ajar, and wasn't an interactable at all despite the engine already having a full grate contract — `type: 'grate'`, `active: true` blocks, `E` sets it false and `InteractionController` swings it open and empties its collider. It now uses that contract. It has to be openable rather than a fixed pane because breach cells are what `forcedOpen` carves to guarantee connectivity, and a permanently sealed one would dead-end a route the pathfinder assumed was walkable. The default open animation rotates the mesh about its own centre and sinks it to y 0.05, which for a panel mounted flush in a wall drives it straight through that wall, so `addGrate` gained a `hinged` mode that hangs the panel off an edge pivot and rotates `pivot.rotation.y` instead. All nine existing vent-grate callers pass four arguments and keep the fall-flat path untouched.
- **[WORLD] The Rubble Breach Variant Is Gone:** The third `WallBreach` type — two canted pillars under a sagging extruded header — has been removed along with `buildSaggingHeader`, its only consumer. Its 30% share is handed to the two survivors in the 0.4 : 0.3 ratio they already had (`FRAME_CUTOFF = 3/7`, measured at 0.5719 / 0.4281 over 2M samples), so breaches stay as frequent as before and only the mix within them changes.
- **[AESTHETICS] Extruded Curves Halved Their Segment Count:** `buildArchCutout`, `buildCurvedCornerBlock` and `curvedFlatGeo` dropped from `curveSegments: 16` to `8`. Their UV math is angle-based rather than segment-indexed, so it's unaffected by the count. See the note below on why this is a polish change and not the performance fix it might look like.
- **[TOOLING] The Insert-Var Toolbar Is One Dropdown:** "Insert Core Var" (a hardcoded `['P','hours','pen','year']`) and "Insert Custom Var" (`paramsData.VARS`) have merged into a single "Insert Var" list covering built-ins and both stores, deduplicated, each showing what it currently resolves to. From inside a document there is no difference worth surfacing — every one of them is a token that resolves to a value — and the split only made the author work out which menu a name lived under before they could insert it. "Insert Role" stays separate.
- **[NARRATIVE] The Redundant Alias Vars Are Deleted:** `PEN` → `ctx.pen`, `EPOCH` → `ctx.siteYear` and `HOUR` → `ctx.hours` existed so a document could write `${PEN}` and match the thread name it was delivering. A count across `lore.json`, `clues.json`, `foreshadow.json`, `finales.json`, `threads.json` and `puzzles.json` found all three used exactly zero times — the documents write `${pen}` (4×), `${year}` (3×) and `${hours}` (1×) instead. Removing them costs nothing and clears three of the five collisions between thread names and variable names. `CIPHER` is kept deliberately: it resolves to the fully-solved access code and must never appear in visible text, but it's wanted for a future easy-mode puzzle that hands the player the answer outright.

### Fixed

- **[WORLD] Baseboards Stopped Growing Inside Special Sectors:** A change to baseboard generation left sector perimeter walls unprotected. `buildWall` gates the baseboard tag on material (`mat === env.sharedWallMat && yOffset === 0`), but `buildPerimeter`'s `pushWallSegment` set `baseboardFootprint` unconditionally, so every sector's perimeter got hallway trim regardless of what it was made of — visible as a yellow-cream baseboard running along the Archive's green wainscoting. Perimeter walls are two-sided, though: outward faces are `sharedWallMat` facing the hallway, inward faces are the sector's own material, and the baseboard is a single box wrapping the whole footprint. Gating on material alone removed it from the hallway side too, breaking the run where a corridor passes an overridden sector. The baseboard now carries per-face material arrays mirroring `multiMat`'s outward/inward logic, so the hallway keeps its continuous run and the interior faces render in the sector's own material.
- **[WORLD] Nothing Blocks An Airlock Approach:** `ChunkManager` already cleared a 3×4 apron in front of every airlock, but only in the wall grid — `setWall(false)` plus `forceStructure(null)`. Once a cell is non-wall it falls through to `_buildEmptyCell`, which has its own spawner, and that rolls a 20% chance of a floor-to-ceiling divider wall. Arteries were exempt; airlock aprons weren't, so a divider could plant itself squarely in the approach the compass is pointing down. The apron calculation is now extracted to `_airlockApron()` — verified behaviour-identical to the inlined version across 33,136 combinations of position, orientation and `outSign` — and shared between the grid clearance and a new `_isAirlockApron()` check that suppresses the divider and its attached chair. Ceiling panels and breakers are untouched so the approach keeps its lighting.
- **[WORLD] The Breach Sill No Longer Stands Proud Of Its Own Wall:** Routing the crawl breach's sill through `buildWall` to give it a baseboard introduced a visible lip: `buildWall` inflates every piece by 0.02 and the jambs and header above were still raw `BoxGeometry`. It also rescales V by `h/3` offset by `yOffset/3`, so the wallpaper tiled at a different frequency above the lip than below it. All three pieces now go through `buildWall` with the correct `yOffset`, giving a continuous V run of 0 → 0.2 → 1.0 and a uniform 0.333 v-units per world unit across the whole wall.
- **[WORLD] Grate Edges Stopped Z-Fighting Their Own Opening:** The closed panel sat 0.02 from the header underside and the sill top, close enough for the depth buffer to argue. Clearance is now 0.06 per edge — a 1.08 square in a 1.2 opening, 0.05 clear on all four sides once `buildWall`'s inflation is accounted for.
- **[NARRATIVE] A Sighting Report Was Printing Its Own Template Token:** `lore.json`'s Chasm sighting report signed off `-${c.lead]` — a closing bracket instead of a brace. The token never closes, so `replaceTemplates` never matched it and the document has been rendering the literal string `-${c.lead]` to the player instead of the lead's name.
- **[TOOLING] Data Validation Catches A Wrong Closing Bracket:** `findTypoTokens` checked for `$(name}` and for a missing `$`, but nothing checked the *closing* side, which is exactly how the malformed token above shipped. It now also flags `]` and `)` closes, verified not to false-positive on valid tokens.

### Notes

- **The curved archways were not the performance problem, and halving their segment count is cosmetic.** Every curved geometry in the kit — `buildArchCutout`, `buildCurvedCornerBlock`, `curvedFlatGeo` — is cached in `env.geoCache` by parameter key, so the `ExtrudeGeometry` construction and its per-vertex UV loop run once per unique key for the entire session, not per cell. Working the selection bands out of `structuralMatrix` (sorted descending, `find(roll >= prob)`) puts `CURVED ARCHWAY` at 0.029–0.031, which is **0.2% of wall cells**; `THE COMPRESSION ARCHWAY` is 0.4%. `ROUND ALCOVE` at 5% is the only curved blueprint common enough to matter. The reduction to 8 segments halves the vertex count of shapes that are built once and shared, which is worth having and is not a fix. If sluggishness persists, the honest next step is instrumenting `geoCache` misses and per-chunk draw calls rather than removing features on suspicion.
- **`HINGED DOORWAY` keeps its probability band even though placement is now entirely from the pre-pass.** The obvious move was to raise `prob` out of range so the random roll could never draw it. Measured, that hands its eight points to the next profile down: `NARROW HEADER GAP` goes from 4% to 12% of all wall cells, tripling silently and reshaping the level. The band therefore stays at 0.78 and a random draw with no plan behind it resolves to plain wall instead, leaving every other blueprint's share exactly as it was. The cost is that those cells are plain wall rather than something, and they don't land in `solidWallCells`, so they aren't offered as breaker mounting points.
- **The doorway planner was developed against a simulation that loads its own source, and that simulation found five bugs that syntax checking could not.** In order: `dir` declared inside the facing-search loop but referenced after it (a hard `ReferenceError` on the first door planted); a later plan's seal walling in an earlier door's approach (4,219 cases); a run winding back to seal its own approach (2,827); the terminus landing on the run's own approach (60); and corridors running alongside another door's approach, letting the player walk in beside a door rather than through it (832, then a further 127 when the run's first cell escaped the same check). All five would have shipped as doors sealed into walls or corridors you could sidestep.
- **Two known limits, both deliberate.** Extending a breach crawl or planting a doorway consumes `random()` draws that the previous code didn't, so chunks containing either will generate differently on an existing seed — the same is already true of the artery exclusion. And the merged VAR screen re-renders the whole list on every edit, because an edit can migrate an entry between stores and change its resolved preview, so focus leaves the field after a change.

## [v0.9.0] - 2026-08-09

_The Airlock Autopsy_

### Fixed

- **[ENGINE] The Sector-Load Freeze Screen Never Actually Covered A Sector Load:** `main.js`'s `LOADING SECTOR...` overlay (added in v0.9.1) only engaged while `environment.isBuildingChunk` was true, and that flag is exclusively managed by `processChunkQueue()` — the ordinary chunk-streaming path. A macro sector's actual interior (the geometry inside an Atrium, Archive, Checkpoint, etc.) builds on a separate, deferred path: `ensurePendingContentAtPlayer()` calls `beginMacroChunkContent()` the moment the player's position crosses into the zone's bounds, and that function fires `_buildChunkInterior()` fire-and-forget, entirely outside `isBuildingChunk`'s bookkeeping. Every sector-entry hitch — including the multi-second Atrium spike below — was therefore invisible to the one system built specifically to explain it: the player just froze mid-frame with no loading state, no cursor change, nothing. `beginMacroChunkContent()` now sets a dedicated `environment.isBuildingMacroInterior` flag for the duration of the build, kept separate from `isBuildingChunk` on purpose since the two can legitimately be in flight at once (neighboring chunks streaming in while the player's own sector interior is still building) and sharing one flag would let whichever finished first drop it out from under the other. `main.js`'s freeze condition now also triggers on this flag directly, independent of the door-transition state the original check required — entering a sector's bounds by any means, airlock included, is reason enough to freeze until its content is actually there to stand on. Verified live: the freeze engages within a frame or two of the interior build starting and releases cleanly the moment it resolves.
- **[WORLD] The Atrium's 14 Balcony Tiers Are Gone:** Every Atrium chunk built one full ring of rails and balusters per tier — 14 tiers, 4 sides each, up to ~27 individual balusters per side on the five nearest tiers — entirely inside the single synchronous cell that also caps the shaft with its void-sky plane. That's on the order of 700 individual `THREE.Mesh`/`THREE.Group` objects constructed back-to-back with no yield point the chunk builder's 5ms time-slicing could land on. They were also only ever visible looking straight up through the airlock opening. Removed the tier construction outright; `TIER_BASE`/`TIER_STEP`/`TIER_COUNT`/`TOP_TIER_Y` stay in place since the perimeter wall banding and the sky cap both still key their height off the same shaft scale. Measured contribution to the sector's build time: negligible on its own (the per-cell work profiled at under 50ms total across an entire chunk), but it's 700 fewer objects and non-instanced draw calls for no visual loss most players will ever notice.

### Notes

- **The balcony removal was not, on its own, the fix for the reported spike.** Isolating the deferred interior build directly (bypassing the fire-and-forget wrapper to await and time it in place) traced the real cost to `ChunkManager._compileInstances`: `renderer.compileAsync` isn't available in this project's three.js build, so its fallback always ends in a synchronous `renderer.compile(env.scene, env.camera)` — a full-scene shader compile, not scoped to the new chunk. Re-verifying already-compiled programs against the rest of the scene is cheap (confirmed: re-running `compile()` on an already-warm scene costs ~20ms regardless of scene size); actually linking a brand-new program is not. The Atrium sector lazily creates roughly a dozen distinct canvas-textured materials — vending panel, cart lattice, soup can, product boxes, and more — that no other sector touches, and the first Atrium a seed ever produces has to compile all of their shaders in one synchronous breath. Measured directly: 3.5-10s of main-thread stall on a fresh Atrium's interior build across five separate samples, against 115-460ms for every other sector and 9-50ms for an ordinary chunk (with an unrelated ~2-3s outlier seen twice on ordinary chunks too, at a rate of roughly 2%, that's a separate open question — see below).
- **Pre-building Atrium's materials at boot was tried and measured to not help.** A `prewarmSectorShaders()` pass built one full, real Atrium chunk in reserved, unreachable coordinates during the boot loading screen specifically to force those dozen materials through `compile()` early, then fully evicted it afterward (including from `discoveredSectors`/`_macroChunkHashes`, which normal chunk eviction deliberately never touches — the Compass reads those to keep pointing at sectors that scrolled out of range, so a leftover fake entry there would eventually point a player at a location a million units out). It added a consistent 8-10s to every boot. Re-tested against a genuine, later, real Atrium entry anyway: still a 10s stall. Compiled shader programs in this three.js build are keyed in part by the live light/shadow state at compile time, which is unavoidably different at boot (before a single real frame has run, every pooled light still at its just-constructed default) than mid-playthrough — so the same material still needed a fresh program on first real entry regardless. Chasing an exact match for that state felt like a good way to trade a known, bounded boot delay for an unproven, fragile fix, so the prewarm was pulled in favor of the freeze-screen fix above: it doesn't make the compile faster, but it makes the wait honest, and it covers every sector's first-entry cost, not just Atrium's.
- **A ~2-3 second stall was also observed on plain, non-sector chunks, independent of Atrium, at roughly a 2% rate across several hundred sampled builds in totally different regions of the seed.** Same underlying mechanism suspected — a chunk whose particular roll of blueprints happens to introduce a material combination the scene hasn't compiled yet — but not yet isolated to a specific blueprint. Worth a follow-up pass with the same isolate-and-time approach used here if it turns out to be a frequent enough player complaint on its own.

## [v0.8.8] - 2026-08-09

_The Load-Bearing Update_

### Added

- **[ENGINE] Sector Doors No Longer Load In Behind Your Back:** Crossing a macro-zone's blast door threshold forces a chunk rebuild for the new sector, and that rebuild was happening in full view — geometry popping in, the Claw and other sector hazards spawning mid-frame, while the player kept full control and could walk straight into architecture that hadn't finished streaming. `Environment.js` now flags `isSectorTransitioning` the moment a door forces a sector, and `main.js` freezes player and input for the duration, fading in a `LOADING SECTOR...` card over a black overlay and holding it until the chunk finishes building, then fading back out over 0.8s. The freeze is a one-shot per transition (`player.wasFrozenByLoad`), so it can't re-trigger mid-fade.
- **[PLAYER] The Flashlight Narrows In The Vents:** A crouch-walk through the crawlspace duct network was still lit like open floor — a full 45m throw and a wide `π/7` cone doing nothing useful in a 1.2m-tall pipe, just blowing out every surface a few feet away. While `player.isCrawling`, angle, penumbra and distance now ease toward a tighter `π/5` cone, 15m throw and a punchier 0.3 penumbra at the same 0.1 lerp rate the beam's intensity already used, so the transition in and out of a duct reads as the beam narrowing to fit the space rather than a hard cut.

### Changed

- **[WORLD] Breach Door Frames Are Visible In Ambient Light:** The intact-breach variant's frame used `pittedMetalMat`/`metalMat` directly, both metalness 0.75+ with no envMap in the scene, which is physically correct PBR but reads as a near-black rendering glitch anywhere the frame lands without a nearby light fixture pushing specular into it — and unlike the props those materials were authored for, a breach can spawn on any wall cell with no such guarantee. A cloned `env.doorFrameMat` at metalness 0.2 / roughness 0.7 keeps it legible under ambient/hemisphere light alone without touching the shared material every other lit prop still uses.
- **[WORLD] Floor-Level Duct Bursts Verify Their Own Exit First:** The floor-level duct opening used to pick a burst length of 1–3 cells at random and then unconditionally force one wall cell open (`ctx.setWall(..., false)`) at the far end, regardless of whether anything was actually on the other side — capable of punching a duct into the solid rock the Drunkard's Walk generator deliberately encases every path in. It now probes up to 5 cells ahead along the tunnel axis for a cell that's already open before committing, and if nothing opens within that search, downgrades to a non-floor-level duct instead of forcing a hole into a dead end.
- **[UI] Terminal Footer Drops The Stale Browse Prompt:** `DocumentViewer.terminalFooter` used to append a `[◄ ► BROWSE RECOVERED FILES]` / `[RE-ACCESS TERMINAL TO BROWSE RECOVERED FILES]` line depending on archive size. The footer is now just the `DATA RECOVERED` count — the browse prompt duplicated what arrow-key navigation already telegraphs the moment a player tries it, and stale copy describing terminal behavior does the reader no favors sitting permanently under every entry.
- **[WORLD] Compression Archway Returns To The Structural Matrix:** Re-added `CompressionArchwayProfile` to `StructuralBlueprints`'s matrix after v0.9.0 pulled it for a low-clearance header that "didn't read clearly in play." The blueprint itself is unchanged from the version that was pulled — its header is still a flat `buildWall` slab, not the new sagging arc introduced above for the wall breach — so this is a straight reinstatement, not a fix to the original complaint. Worth watching in play; if the header still doesn't read, the sagging-header treatment is the more likely fix than another removal.

### Fixed

- **[SYSTEM] Story Progress Actually Survives A Save:** `SaveManager` previously wrote nothing about read documents, corroborated threads, or dealt tapes/laptops/clipboards into `level0_state` — reloading a save reset the entire case file. `StoryEngine` gained `exportState()`/`importState()` covering `readTemplates`, `collected`, `assignments`, `trackers`, `cycleIndex`, `tapesDealt`, `ephemeraDealt`, `laptopsDealt`, `clipboardsDealt`, `corroborated` and `sectorsRead`, wired through `SaveManager.saveState()`/`loadState()`. The first pass at the load side called `environment.getStory().importState(...)` from inside `loadState()`, which runs *before* `environment.setup()` derives `baseSeed` from the just-restored seed input — `getStory()` caches its `StoryEngine` instance per `baseSeed`, so the import landed on a throwaway instance keyed to the pre-setup seed and was silently discarded the moment the real seed took over on the next `getStory()` call. `loadState()` no longer touches story state at all; `main.js` now imports it explicitly after `environment.setup()` resolves and before the post-load `updateChunks()` call that would otherwise be the first thing to prime the cache.
- **[UI] Interrupting The Typewriter No Longer Closes The Page:** Both `DocumentViewer` and `JournalViewer` bind their reveal-completion and their close action to the same key. Pressing it while a document or journal entry was still typing itself out used to close the whole overlay outright, since the handler only ever checked `isReading`/`isOpen` and never looked at whether a typewriter was still running — losing the rest of the text and requiring a second read to see it. The close handler in each now checks for an active `typeWriterInterval` first: if one is running, it clears the interval, snaps the pane straight to the full text, and consumes that press instead of closing anything. A second press closes as before.
- **[ENTITY] The Claw Reset Its Own Clock:** `ClawEntity.activate()` reset `state` and `idleTimer` on reuse but left `warningTimer`, `dropTimer`, `snapTimer` and `ascentTimer` holding whatever values they'd reached at the end of the *previous* encounter, so a re-triggered Claw could inherit a `warningTimer` already past its 1.2s threshold and skip straight into a drop with no warning telegraph. All four now reset alongside the rest of the encounter state. Separately, the idle-to-warning transition now also checks `!environment.isBuildingChunk`, so the Claw can't clock the player as motionless and start a drop sequence while a chunk (and the player's control) is frozen for the sector-load screen above.

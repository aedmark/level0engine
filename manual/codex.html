The Three Standard Questions

Every sector, blueprint, hazard, and tool in this codex is broken down into three concrete realities: What It Does (sensory presentation, gameplay effects, and player interactions), How It Works (algorithmic generation, spatial registration, and mathematical pipelines), and Why It Works (psychological design rationale, performance bounds, and zero-allocation constraints).

Volume Status

The reference tables in this volume are generated from the source, and the entries were checked against it during the v1.5.8.9 review. The systems still waiting for an entry are listed in the outline without a link, and completing them is tracked as P4-03 in ROADMAP.md.

Normal : The Mono-Yellow Corridors

Macro Sector Baseline Hub
What It Does

The foundational starting environment of Level 0. Damp wallpaper, musty carpet, buzzing 60Hz fluorescent tubes, and sprawling standard halls that connect the complex.

How It Works

Acts as the default cell archetype in SectorPlacement.js. Synthesizes procedural carpet and wallpaper textures via ProceduralTextureFactory.js. Integrates StandardHall, AlcoveCorner, and Duct blueprints.

Why It Works

Provides the iconic liminal reference point. Its visual monotony lulls the player into disorientation, making sudden transitions into hazardous sectors jarring and terrifying.

Archive : The Paper Labyrinth

Macro Sector Dust Haze Entity: Archivist
What It Does

Dense stacks of metal document shelves rising to the ceiling, sorting tables, and scattered case files under dim, sepia-toned dust fog.

How It Works

Instantiates ArchiveSector.js, injecting high-density document spawn hooks into NarrativeProps.js. Muffles high frequencies via AcousticEngine to mimic paper sound absorption.

Why It Works

The primary research hub. Contains crucial cold-case documents, but tight sightlines and rustling paper make evading the sound-sensitive Archivist treacherous.

Atrium : Vertical Courtyards

Macro Sector Multi-Tier Verticality Echo Acoustics
What It Does

Expansive three-story courtyards with skylight grids, concrete balconies, and central elevator shafts that puncture the endless low-ceiling claustrophobia.

How It Works

AtriumSector.js clears ceiling cells across a 4×4 chunk footprint, placing modular perimeter balconies and open railings. AcousticEngine maximizes reverb time to 3.8 seconds.

Why It Works

Offers sudden scale shock. Wide visibility gives false safety, but footstep echoes carry across great distances, alerting distant predators across multiple floors.

Exit : The Subterranean Bunker

Macro Sector Victory Chamber Inquest Terminal
What It Does

Heavy blast bunker containing the Inquest Terminal, staff ephemera, and the locked industrial hoist elevator to the surface.

How It Works

ExitSector.js generates a sealed bunker sealed behind a reinforced blast airlock. Activates only when all surge breakers are thrown, orienting the Threshold Compass directly to its coordinates.

Why It Works

Acts as the climatic culmination of the gameplay loop, requiring physical maze traversal, evidence collation, and PIN deciphering.

ArchHall : Vaulted Arch Hallway

Blueprint Prob: 0.0800 Subway Tile Trim
What It Does

Replaces plain rectangular corridors with an elegant, vaulted plaster archway adorned with ceramic subway border tiling and recessed overhead fixtures.

How It Works

Constructed via StructureKit.createArchMesh(). Inherits material parameters from the active sector registry and aligns seamless UV coords with adjacent corridors.

Why It Works

Breaks visual monotony without altering collision boundaries, indicating transitional conduits between commercial and industrial sections.

CrawlspaceDuct : Low-Clearance Conduit

Blueprint Prob: 0.0350 Requires `Q`
What It Does

A cramped metal conduit dropping ceiling height to 1.1 meters, forcing the player to compress (`Q`) to squeeze through.

How It Works

Generates low horizontal ceiling panels and registers a low bounding box in SpatialHashGrid. Prevents player traversal unless camera height is crouched.

Why It Works

Induces severe claustrophobia while serving as an escape choke-point against larger entities that cannot fit through the aperture.

TheFaradayCage : Copper Sanctuary

Blueprint Prob: 0.0014 Safe Zone
What It Does

A rare copper-clad refuge room with a green CRT terminal, a flickering overhead fixture, and a spatial blocker that keeps entities out.

How It Works

Walls one cell in 0.4-thick copper, leaving a single 1.2-wide opening, then inserts a full-cell, 3 m-tall isEntityBlocker box into env.spatialGrid so entity pathing cannot enter.

Why It Works

Provides critical psychological contrast: true relief only exists when the threat outside is absolute.

👁️ Entities & Hazards (8 Systems · 2 Written)

Entities are autonomous hazards managed by EntityManager.js. They use preallocated scratch vectors, raycast line-of-sight checks, and acoustic hearing models.

Anomaly : The Roaming Predator

Global Hazard Line-of-Sight Light-Sensitive
What It Does

The primary horror force of Level 0. Roams the facility hunting by sound. Freezes while lit by the flashlight at close range, but builds rage the longer it is observed, which makes it faster once the beam drops.

How It Works
  1. Sensory Model: Calculates player noise levels against hearing distance. Exhaustion and sprinting amplify detection ranges.
  2. Cone Check: Compares the camera's forward vector against the direction to the entity. With the flashlight on, clear line of sight, distance under 25 m, and a dot product above 0.85, velocity is set to zero.
  3. Aberration FX: Drives dynamic post-processing shaders (scanlines, vignette, chromatic aberration) proportional to proximity.
Why It Works

Creates heart-pounding staredowns where looking directly at death keeps you alive, until your battery begins to die.

SurgeBreaker : High-Voltage Power Lever

Interactive Hazard Input: `E` Blackout Cascade
What It Does

Breaker podiums in Maintenance. Throwing one opens the podium's door and blacks out its chunk, dropping the corridors fed by that circuit into darkness.

How It Works

InteractionController.triggerBreaker() opens the podium door, adds the chunk hash to env.blackoutChunks, and fires the somatic-breaker event. Inside Maintenance a randomized 25–35 second timer restores power to that chunk.

Why It Works

Forces the player to choose between progression and visibility, transforming familiar hallways into pitch-black hunting grounds.

🔦 Survival Equipment & Tools (4 Systems · 2 Written)

Flashlight : Kinetic Dyno-Lantern

Essential Gear Cranked Recharge Toggle: `F`
What It Does

Heavy handheld lantern casting a narrow spot beam. Recharged mechanically by sprinting or shaking the camera view.

How It Works

Flashlight.js monitors player displacement and camera delta rotations in SomaticInput.js, adding charge units up to the paranoia battery ceiling.

Why It Works

Transforms illumination into active resource management. Running out of light in Level 0 is a death sentence.

Compass : The Threshold Magnetometer

Navigation Input: `M` Sector Seeking
What It Does

A brass handheld compass raised with `M`. Its needle points toward the nearest unvisited macro-sector blast door, or towards the Exit Bunker.

How It Works

Calculates the heading vector toward nearest sector coordinates extracted from SectorPlacement.js and rotates the 3D needle mesh smoothly.

Why It Works

Prevents players from getting hopelessly lost in an infinite fractal while preserving diegetic in-world navigation.

🎛️ Developer Tools & Tuners (7 Systems · 1 Written)

AtmosphereTuner : Live Volumetric Tuner

Dev Tool Source Rewriting Port 8080 POST
What It Does

Live in-game UI displaying sliders for fog near/far distances, fog colors, and atmospheric drag, with a button to save values directly to Sectors.js.

How It Works

Modifies active THREE.Fog instances in real-time. Dispatches JSON payloads to /save-atmosphere on engine_server.js which regex-updates the source file.

Why It Works

Enables instant artistic iteration on horror lighting and fog moods without stopping the dev server or reloading the browser.
